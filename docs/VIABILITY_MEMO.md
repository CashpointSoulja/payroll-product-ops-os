# Viability memo: Staff Product Operations Manager, FinTech (Deel)

**Author:** Ayo Ahmed · **Date:** 19 October 2026
**Question:** Is it realistic to compete for this seat, and what does this artifact do (and not do) for that case?

## 1. What the seat asks for

From the public job description:

- Lead cross-functional product projects; **collect, quantify and prioritise** product challenges and user feedback.
- **Drive go-to-market** strategy and global rollouts for payroll and financial products.
- **Root-cause** product issues with engineering.
- **Design operational processes** between fintech teams (FinOps, FinCrime, TechOps) and banking partners.
- Requirements: **7+ years in product operations**, **3+ years in financial products** (e.g. wallets, PSP, AML, cards), strong **SQL and BI** (e.g. Looker, Snowflake), stakeholder management in a fast-moving, product-led company. Integration knowledge is a plus.

"Staff" means the person is expected to set the operating model for others, not just run it. They also need to be trusted in rooms with legal, finance and engineering leads on day one.

## 3. What this artifact is for

It does not replace years of experience. It reduces the hiring team's uncertainty about the things a CV can't show:

1. **How I think about prioritisation:** a published model with a compliance floor and logged overrides, rather than "I'm data-driven".
2. **How I'd run a payroll launch:** per-country readiness with money movement (funding, reclaim, payouts) as a first-class workstream, which is where payroll and fintech meet.
3. **How I root-cause:** competing hypotheses, rejected ones kept visible, "verified" kept separate from "shipped".
4. **How I'd run the week:** rituals with fixed inputs, written outputs and a digest.
5. **Execution speed and finish:** a deployed, tested, documented working tool, not a slide.

## 5. What would close the gap fastest

- **Domain:** working depth on payroll funding flows, payout rails (SEPA, Faster Payments, ACH), returns and reconciliation, and how KYC/AML controls create product friction. The sample data deliberately includes these (FB-1066, FB-1070, FB-1072, RCA-038) to show where I'd focus.
- **Data:** a public SQL portfolio piece on a realistic payments or payroll dataset that reproduces the scorecard in [METRICS.md](METRICS.md).
- **Evidence from my own operations:** one written case with before/after numbers from the businesses I run (e.g. how follow-up automation changed conversion to recurring bookings), written in the same RCA format used here.
